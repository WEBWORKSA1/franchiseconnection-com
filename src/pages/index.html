    The franchise buying path, in the order it happens

    Most expensive mistakes come from doing these out of order — signing before validating, or shopping before knowing your number.

    1. Know your number

      Liquid capital, net worth, and the monthly income you need while the business ramps. Affordability check

    2. Shortlist categories

      Pick two or three industries that fit your budget, skills and the hours you want to work. Explorer

    3. Read the FDD

      Items 7, 19, 20 and 21 tell you cost, earnings, closures and franchisor health. FDD guide

    4. Call franchisees

      Validation calls with current and former owners are the best data you will get. 50 questions

    5. Line up financing

      SBA 7(a), ROBS, home equity or franchisor programs — and the cash you must inject. Financing guide

    6. Sign with counsel

      A franchise attorney reviews the agreement; the FTC requires 14 calendar days with the FDD first. Full walkthrough

    Common questions

    Is FranchiseConnection.com free for franchise buyers?

    Yes. The tools, guides and match report are free. We earn from advertising, from franchisors who pay to reach qualified buyers, and from optional reader support. That never changes the category data, which is brand-neutral.

    Are you a franchise broker?

    No. We are an independent information and matching service. If you ask for introductions, we may connect you with franchisors or with franchise consultants and lenders. We will tell you when a partner pays us for an introduction.

    Where do the investment ranges come from?

    They are typical bands observed across Item 7 of published Franchise Disclosure Documents for each category. Individual brands sit above or below. Always use the specific brand's current FDD. Read our editorial policy.

    How much money do I need to buy a franchise?

    Lenders usually expect you to inject 20–30% of the total project in cash, keep a personal reserve, and have a net worth well above the loan. Home-based service franchises can start under $100,000 all-in; food and fitness formats often run $300,000 to well over $1 million. Try the affordability check.

    Do you cover Canada, the UK and Australia?

    Yes. Category economics are shown in US dollars, and our international law guide covers the FTC Rule, Canadian provincial franchise acts, the UK's self-regulation and Australia's Franchising Code.
