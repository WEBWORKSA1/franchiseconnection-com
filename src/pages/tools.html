How to get honest numbers into these calculators

A calculator is only as good as the revenue figure you type into it. Franchise sales teams naturally talk about their best locations, so build your inputs from three independent places.

Item 7 of the FDD gives the franchisor's own low and high estimate of your initial investment. Use the high end for anything involving construction, and add the contingency the calculator suggests. Item 19, when a franchisor provides one, is the only place a franchisor may legally state what units earn. Look for the median, not the average, and check how many units the figures cover and whether closed units were excluded. Franchisee validation calls close the gap: ask owners what they spent, how long it took to break even and what a typical month looks like now.

Labor and rent are where most plans go wrong. Get a real lease quote for your target area and price labor at local wages plus payroll taxes and benefits. If the business only works when the owner works full-time unpaid, that is an important finding, not a rounding error.

None of these tools give financial, legal or tax advice. Use them to prepare better questions for your accountant, lender and franchise attorney.
